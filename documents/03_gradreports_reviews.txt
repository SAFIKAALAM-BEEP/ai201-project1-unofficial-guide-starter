Shah | Degree: Psychology | Graduation Year: 2025 | Reviewed: 1/15/2026
Hunter is a huge school and a commuter school. This means that it's slightly harder to make friends and you need to really put the effort in unless you are part of some sort of honors program which gives you a community. There is a wide range of clubs that do great events though and you have to put yourself out there. Due to the size of the school, it can be hard to get into the classes you want without priority enrollment. The classes themselves are okay depending on the professors. Some classes are notoriously hard with terrible professors, so you really have to lock in or ePermit to another CUNY if the circumstances allow. I recommend this school only if you can get in through some program such as the Honors programs, Macaulay Honors College, or you get priority enrollment status in some way (USG member, Thomas Hunter student).

Anonymous | Degree: Biology | Graduation Year: 2025 | Reviewed: 1/15/2026
CUNY Hunter College has really good value. You receive a quality education for a low price. There are numerous resources, departments, majors, and clubs that encourage students to be part of the community. For example, the career center has a professional personally talk with you and find out what you want in your career. They help you fix your resume and give you recommendations on internships, or ways to boost your marketability in your field. They often host events with snacks and coffee to encourage students to come. Hunter doesn't fit traditional college life in some aspects — they don't have a big outdoor campus, they are part of the city, which can also be seen as a pro for many people.

butter | Degree: Computer Science | Graduation Year: 2021 | Reviewed: 1/14/2026
Hunter college was an okay experience for me as a computer science graduate. The school was located in the city which was convenient for commuting and internships. The computer science program gave me a basic foundation in programming and problem solving. I learned languages like Java, Python, and some web development tools. Some professors were helpful and explained concepts clearly; others were less organized and hard to understand. I had to teach myself many topics using online resources. Career support was available but not very strong — I had to search for opportunities on my own. The city offered many tech events and job opportunities which helped me stay motivated. The degree was practical for entry-level roles.

Brian B | Degree: Biology | Graduation Year: 2019 | Reviewed: 9/21/2019

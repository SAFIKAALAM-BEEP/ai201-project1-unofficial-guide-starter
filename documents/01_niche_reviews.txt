--- SUMMARY (AI-generated from review text) ---
CUNY Hunter College offers a diverse and academically rigorous environment that many students find rewarding, particularly due to passionate professors and a wide range of course offerings. The college's urban location in Manhattan is seen as a significant advantage, providing students with access to numerous internship opportunities and cultural experiences. However, students frequently mention challenges with administration, including difficulties navigating advising and support services that can affect their academic journeys. Many reviews highlight the social life as being less vibrant, largely due to the commuter student demographic, which can make it hard to build connections outside of the classroom. While many appreciate the affordability and educational quality at Hunter, concerns about outdated facilities and inconsistent communication from staff indicate areas needing improvement.

Rating: 4/5 | Year: Senior | Posted: recently
I enjoyed the professors immensely. Most of my classes were within the social sciences and humanities, and I did not come across any professor that didn't care about their subject. I would like to see a broader range of classes at various times. I couldn't take a lot of the 4–5 pm classes due to scheduling constraints, and within my 4 years I never saw them offered in the morning. I also believe there needs to be more classes generally, especially language classes. We're required to take 4 semesters of a single language to graduate, with each class progressing in difficulty. Yet even for juniors the language courses feel inaccessible because they fill up so quickly. That requirement either needs to be amended or there need to be more language courses available.

Rating: 3/5 | Year: Freshman | Posted: 2 months ago
The school has been manageable. I did drop a class due to the most incompetent professor available for that specific course. Everything is online via an application called Top Hat, which is similar to Google Classroom or Canvas. The catch is that for this specific class you need to buy a textbook e-book — an access code — written by the professor themselves, and it has a lot of mistakes. The advising office is very helpful and resourceful; please use it. Not every student is assigned their own advisor if you are an incoming freshman, so there are peer advisors you can just walk in and speak with. They're very helpful for recommending degree progress and helping you find classes for the following semester. Office hours are very useful. Food is okay.
